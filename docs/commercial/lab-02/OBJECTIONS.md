# Objection cards

Role-play prompts for a hiring discussion, not transcripts or customer endorsements. Acknowledge the concern, test the assumption, and agree evidence before recommending a product.

## “We already use GitHub.”

**Response:** “What works well, and which delivery or evidence problem remains unresolved? If today's setup meets your requirements, switching may not be worthwhile.”

**Ask:** Which improvement would matter enough to justify change?

**Next step:** Compare the same [acceptance criteria](PILOT_SCORECARD.md), including a retain-GitHub option.

**Avoid:** Dismissing existing investment or claiming GitHub lacks integrated security.

## “Your lab proves GitLab is more secure.”

**Response:** “It shows configured checks passed for one application and commit. It is not a platform security ranking or production assurance.”

**Ask:** Which threat, control, and evidence workflow need evaluation?

**Next step:** Use a representative service and [verify the required entitlements](COMPARISON.md).

**Avoid:** Calling external CLI scans a test of paid GitLab security products.

## “Will this save money?”

**Response:** “Only a baseline and complete quote can establish that. We should include ongoing ownership, migration, training, and commitments we cannot retire.”

**Ask:** Is the goal cash reduction, team capacity, risk evidence, or a combination?

**Next step:** Fill the [low/base/high cost template](COST_MODEL.md) with buyer-owned evidence.

**Avoid:** Presenting Lab 01's hypothetical capacity value as measured savings or platform ROI.

## “Can your scanners stop every vulnerability?”

**Response:** “No. Coverage, advisory freshness, configuration, and human triage all matter.”

**Ask:** Who accepts residual risk, and how are exceptions reviewed and expired?

**Next step:** Agree blocking policy, a safe synthetic failure, and a named exception owner.

**Avoid:** Claiming a passing scan is proof of absence of vulnerabilities.

## “Migration is too risky.”

**Response:** “That may rule out a change. Let's establish what cannot be disrupted before we consider a pilot.”

**Ask:** Which integrations, policies, data, or developer workflows must remain intact?

**Next step:** Limit any evaluation to one non-production service with a stop/rollback plan.

**Avoid:** Promising a seamless migration without testing or dismissing sunk and committed costs.

## “We do not have budget or time for another tool.”

**Response:** “Then a broad evaluation may be the wrong next step. Is there a documented gap important enough to justify even a small discovery exercise?”

**Ask:** What is the decision horizon, responsible owner, and funding path?

**Next step:** Defer with an agreed condition for revisiting, or close the evaluation when no case exists.

**Avoid:** Manufacturing urgency, inventing a sponsor, or proposing paid features without an authorised scope.
