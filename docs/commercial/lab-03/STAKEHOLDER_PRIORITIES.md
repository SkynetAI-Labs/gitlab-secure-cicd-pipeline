# Stakeholder priorities and tensions

These are **hypothesised priorities**, not buyer statements. Read the [scenario](SCENARIO.md) and [personas](ENTERPRISE_BUYERS.md) first. This page translates differences into evidence and decisions; it does not repeat Lab 02's discovery cards or objections.

| Role | Possible desired outcome | Evidence to prepare or validate | Trade-off to make explicit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 · Executive / economic authority | A prioritised business outcome with accountable ownership | Buyer-confirmed problem, alternatives and decision basis | Benefit versus the disruption and opportunity cost of change |
| R2 · Finance | A fundable case with complete costs | Quote scope, commitments, cash impact and [existing cost model](../lab-02/COST_MODEL.md) | Economic capacity value versus actual retireable spend |
| R3 · Platform | Manageable standardisation and operating effort | Ownership plan, integrations, maintenance and migration scope | Common evidence versus central-team burden |
| R4 · Architecture | Compatibility with mandatory requirements | Buyer architecture criteria and authorised technical evidence | Desired capability versus actual infrastructure constraints |
| R5 · AppSec / risk | Reviewable evidence and clear residual-risk ownership | Agreed controls, findings workflow, exception approvals | More findings or reports versus effective triage and governance |
| R6 · Legal / privacy | A reviewable contractual/data-handling arrangement | Required documents and unresolved issues, assessed by authorised reviewers | Preferred timeline versus completion of necessary review |
| R7 · Procurement | A permitted and executable purchase | Vendor record, contracting route, purchasing dependencies and signer | A preferred supplier versus an approved purchasing route |
| R8 · Service teams | A workable daily process with support | Usability acceptance, training and ongoing ownership | Standardisation versus effective practices already in use |
| R9 · Partner, if relevant | Clear responsibilities and compatible incentives | Buyer-confirmed partner route, service scope and accountability | Useful delivery support versus duplicated cost or unclear ownership |

## Three conflicts to resolve in the role-play

1. **Platform consistency and service autonomy.** Use a representative workflow and buyer-approved constraints to identify what must be common and what can remain team-owned. Reuse the [Lab 02 pilot scorecard](../lab-02/PILOT_SCORECARD.md); do not declare standardisation beneficial by default.
2. **Security evidence and assumed product entitlement.** Distinguish generic CI reports from the specific reporting workflow the buyer needs. The selected [current product facts](SOURCE_REGISTER.md) must be checked against the actual configuration and quote. Lab 01's CLI results do not establish paid-feature access or security-review acceptance.
3. **Executive momentum and unconfirmed funding/reviews.** Make funding, legal/procurement dependencies and review ownership visible before discussing an executable purchase date. No seller-imposed urgency or imaginary approval lead time belongs in the plan.

For each tension, record the accountable buyer roles, decision criterion, supporting evidence and unresolved condition. If objectives conflict, make the trade-off visible for an authorised buyer decision rather than promise every stakeholder an incompatible outcome. This is a proposed facilitation approach; no alignment session or agreement took place.
